# Code of Conduct

## The rule

Be civil. Assume competence and good faith. Criticise the work, not the
person.

That is the whole of it. The rest of this document is elaboration for the
cases where it is not obvious, and a description of what happens when someone
ignores it.

## What this looks like in practice

**Address the change, not the contributor.** "This breaks on big-endian
targets" is useful. "Did you even test this?" is not, and the first version
takes no longer to write.

**Say why.** A rejected contribution with a reason teaches something. A
rejected contribution without one just reads as arbitrary.

**Take correction well.** This repository has a documentation suite whose
entire purpose is to prove the prose wrong, and it has already found six
incorrect claims written by the person who wrote the suite. Being wrong in
public is the normal condition here.

**Read before objecting.** [README section 3.1](README.md#31-non-features)
lists the features that have been proposed and declined, and
[INSTRUCTIONS.md Part 15](INSTRUCTIONS.md#part-15-troubleshooting) covers
fifteen common failures. Both exist so that the same conversation does not
have to happen repeatedly.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, in issues, pull requests, or private contact
  arising from this project
- Personal attacks, insults, or demeaning comments
- Publishing anyone's private information without their permission
- Sustained disruption of discussion, or bad-faith argument
- Sexual attention or advances

None of these are matters of tone or taste. They are grounds for removal.

## A note specific to this project

The documentation here is aimed partly at people writing their first program.
[INSTRUCTIONS.md](INSTRUCTIONS.md) opens by assuming the reader does not own a
computer, and closes with an appendix about frustration.

Condescension toward beginners is therefore out of place in this repository in
a way it might not be elsewhere. Everyone in this field was once stuck for an
hour on a typographic quotation mark. Nobody arrived knowing what `./` was
for, or how to exit `vim`.

## Reporting

Report privately using GitHub's report feature, or by contacting a maintainer
directly. Do not open a public issue about someone's conduct; it escalates the
problem rather than resolving it.

Reports are handled discreetly. The reporter's identity is not disclosed to
the person reported.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, remove, or reject any contribution, and may warn,
temporarily block, or permanently ban any participant. In practice the
response is proportionate and escalates only if the behaviour does:

1. A private note explaining what was wrong
2. A warning
3. A temporary block
4. A permanent ban

Serious cases — harassment, threats, publishing private information — skip
straight to the last step.

## Scope

This applies to issues, pull requests, commit messages, code review, and any
other space belonging to this project, as well as to conduct elsewhere that is
directed at participants because of their involvement here.
